*This essay was written in Spanish. What follows is a translation; the [original](/es/blog/por-que-escribo-en-espanol/) is the one the argument is about.*

English has given me a lot, which is why this decision is not obvious. But this blog is going to be in Spanish. There is a little fatigue with English, yes. But it is more of a longing: the wish to enrich my soul with what I identify with.

## What I owe English

I started reading in English at 15. I thought it would be a great way to learn the language better, read faster, broaden my horizons, and find information I could not find in Spanish. One of those first books was on economics, the kind of content that was not easy to find in Spanish. I also knew I would use English professionally, and that motivated me a lot.

English does open a world of opportunities. Programming happens in English. If you look for a YouTube video to learn something, there is far more content in English, and many business books are still not translated. The numbers bear it out: almost half of all websites are in English and only 6% in Spanish, according to [W3Techs](https://w3techs.com/technologies/history_overview/content_language/ms/y) (2026).

Today I am studying for a master's degree in English at a university in the United States. American universities have more years, more legacy, and in certain respects they are better. English has given me the chance to work remotely for the United States, to be a programmer, and to enjoy books I otherwise could not have read.

## The flavor of a language

But there came a point, after many years, when reading became something I do for pleasure. And in English I do not always find the same pleasure. It does not taste the same.

My whole world here in Vallarta is in Spanish. I do speak English now and then with a Canadian or American neighbor, but my best friends are Mexican, my family is Mexican, and my identity is Mexican. And not only Mexican: Hispanic.

I have been to Spain and to Ecuador, and from the moment I set foot in Spanish-speaking territory I feel I never left home. I feel very present. I can greet people with the familiarity of my own country, make friends, and share emotions. On those trips I met people and places that gave me a warmth and treated me with a fraternity like no other, and I still hold many of those people dear today. We share the same culture and a very similar way of thinking, of communicating, and of seeing the world.

That is why I enjoy reading in Spanish much more. It is a richer read, with more personality for my taste and my feelings. Right now I am reading *One Thousand and One Nights*. It was not written in Spanish and it is an enormously long book, but I am having a lot of fun; the stories resonate with me more than they would in English. I cannot read it in Arabic, so Spanish is the closest I have to that region and that culture.

I think it works like food. We are biased by the way we grew up: our mother's seasoning shapes our taste, and the seasoning of our language shapes the way we see the world.

## I think differently in Spanish

Yes, I think differently in Spanish. In English I go straight to a more professional way of thinking, because that is where my training is. Even my personality changes. In Spanish I think less about business and more about life: about philosophy, about love, about friendship, about living together.

When an English word slips out, it is because I cannot find the word in Spanish. There is surely a Spanish word that describes what I mean better, but my Spanish vocabulary is sometimes limited and the English word is the first one that comes to me. While dictating this piece, *longing* slipped out. The word I was looking for was *anhelo*.

## Not more people: the right people

Yes, in English I would reach more people. But in English there are also more people talking, more people trying to reach you. The difference shows when you measure per person: English is about 26% of internet users and Spanish about 8% ([Internet World Stats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_used_on_the_Internet), 2020). With the W3Techs data, that works out to about two and a half times more content per user in English than in Spanish.

On the other side is the reader. In a [CSA Research](https://csa-research.com/blogs-events/csa-in-the-media/press-releases/consumers-prefer-their-own-language) survey (2020) of 8,709 consumers in 29 countries, 76% preferred to buy products with information in their own language and 40% said they never buy from websites in another language. And in Mexico English is not the norm: the country ranked 103rd of 123 in the [EF English Proficiency Index](https://www.ef.com/wwes/epi/) (2025), last in Latin America.

I do not want to reach more people, but the right people: those my words will have the greatest impact on. And if someone prefers English, since February 2026 [YouTube automatically dubs](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/unlocking-a-global-audience-with-auto-dubbing/) videos into 27 languages, with a voice that tries to keep the emotion of the original.

## The open door

I choose to write in Spanish for those who, like me, are looking to connect in a more personal way. I do not write only to strengthen my professional profile and my job prospects. I write to share, genuinely and with no agenda, what I am: lessons, things learned, and failures, with complete transparency. If any of this echoes with someone, if it amuses them, helps them, or inspires them, that is all I need. That is my biggest motivation: that someone connects with what I say.

I want people to take a part of me from what I am writing now and from whatever I write from here on. I have no social media outside LinkedIn and YouTube, so I want them to know I am not in the dark: that I am still here, giving it everything just as they are, and that they can connect with me at any time.

What I want is for us to inspire and support each other, to create, to share, and to have fun. My door is always open.
